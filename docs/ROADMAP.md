# From prototype to research pilot

## v0.1 — Local demonstrator (implemented)

- Seven milestones with explanations, examples, and artifact templates.
- Required student reasoning and local supervisor decision records.
- Version-specific approvals and downstream invalidation.
- Manual sources, chronological activity, artifact versions, and exports.
- Deterministic demo or optional three-call Ollama guidance sequence.
- Local atomic persistence and automated transition/API tests.

## v0.2 — A real pilot in one discipline

Recruit beginning PhD students and supervisors before increasing scope. Review the templates with a domain-methods specialist. Choose a licensed public dataset and a narrow, answerable question.

- Make explanations respond to demonstrated understanding; evaluate with independent pre/post tasks.
- Add full-project import, switching, deletion, and recovery controls.
- Separate researcher and supervisor roles with authentication and server authorization.
- Define evidence and output references that can be checked, not just pasted.
- Add provider capabilities, cancellation, streamed run events, and explicit retry budgets.

## v0.3 — Tool-using research execution

- Retrieve authorized literature and record source provenance and scope limits.
- Profile a supported public dataset without treating its contents as instructions.
- Execute an approved R analysis in an isolated, resource-limited environment.
- Store executable artifacts and compare results with manuscript claims.
- Add coordinator tool selection and replanning with software-enforced approvals.

## Evaluation

Evaluate research quality separately from student learning. Measure methodological errors, evidence support, reproducibility, supervisor correction time, model cost, and students' ability to justify choices without assistance. Compare against ordinary tools and a single-agent baseline. Record failure cases and false alarms. A polished artifact or agreement among agents is not sufficient evidence of competence or rigor.

## Startup validation

Interview group leaders and doctoral-program directors about actual projects, correction effort, purchasing authority, and existing alternatives. Test paid pilots only after defining a useful deliverable. A demonstration and friendly feedback do not establish willingness to pay.
